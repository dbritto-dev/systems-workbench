Systems lesson 23 · Health checks

Health Probe Workbench

Kubernetes asks every pod two different questions: can you take traffic right now (readiness), and are you so broken that only a restart will help (liveness)? Mixing them up turns small problems into outages. This lesson builds that idea three ways: watch a machine do it, predict what breaks it, then explain it back.

02

Predict, then run

Each experiment changes one thing. Choose what you expect before running it. Results come from the same model as the machine, averaged over four seeded runs; probes run every 3 s.

1. No readiness probe

Experiment 1 of 5
A deploy restarts shops 4 and 5 under steady traffic · readiness probe on → off
Each restarted shop takes 12 s to open its doors and 10 s to stock its shelves. Without a readiness probe, what do customers see?

Without a readiness probe, Kubernetes adds a pod to the service as soon as its container starts. The warden sends customers to a shop whose doors aren't open yet (connection refused) and then to empty shelves (cold caches, no connections). A readiness probe that only says yes once the shop can really serve keeps it off the list until then.

2. Liveness checks the warehouse

Experiment 2 of 5
The warehouse (database) is down for 30 s · liveness checks the warehouse vs readiness checks it
With the liveness probe checking the warehouse, when is the street fully open again?

Restarting a shop can't fix a warehouse. When liveness depends on it, every inspector sees the same failure at the same time and rebuilds every shop at once. The warehouse comes back to a street of scaffolding, and shops that restart again get a growing back-off. Checking the warehouse in readiness only takes shops off the warden's list for the duration and puts them back as soon as it returns.

3. A tight timeout on a busy street

Experiment 3 of 5
Traffic at 90% of capacity · liveness timeout 5 s → 1 s
The shops are slow but healthy: probes wait in the same queue as customers. What does a 1 s liveness timeout do?

A busy shop answers its inspector late, not never. With a 1 s timeout, three late answers in a row look like a dead shop, so it gets rebuilt. Its queue is lost, and its customers move to the other shops, which become even busier and miss their own checks. Give liveness a generous timeout and threshold: it should catch a process that is truly stuck, not one that is busy.

4. A slow start

Experiment 4 of 5
A shop takes 60 s to open its doors · liveness starts checking 10 s after start · no startup probe vs a startup probe
Without a startup probe, when is the restarted shop ready?

Liveness starts checking 10 s after the container starts and gives up after three failures, about 20 s in. A shop that needs 60 s is killed every time, and the back-off between attempts keeps growing: a crash loop. A startup probe holds liveness and readiness back until the shop answers once (here allowing up to 120 s), then normal checks take over.

5. A jammed shop

Experiment 5 of 5
Shop 3 jams: its doors are open but nobody answers · readiness only vs readiness and liveness
With only a readiness probe, what happens to shop 3?

Readiness only decides whether customers are sent there. The jammed shop drops off the warden's list, so errors stop, but nothing ever fixes it: you run on four shops until someone notices. Liveness exists for exactly this: a process that is stuck in a way only a restart can fix.

03

Explain it back

Answer out loud or in writing before opening each one. These work well as a pairing exercise: one person explains, the other asks "why?" twice.

Q1In one sentence each: what does a readiness probe decide, and what does a liveness probe decide?
A good answer coversReadiness decides whether the pod gets traffic: failing removes it from the service's endpoints, and nothing is restarted. Liveness decides whether the container should be killed and restarted: failing it enough times restarts the container. Readiness is "not right now"; liveness is "this is broken and a restart will fix it".
Q2Your liveness probe calls the database. Why is that dangerous?
A good answer coversA database outage makes every pod's liveness fail at once, so Kubernetes restarts all of them. The database comes back to pods that are still booting and warming up, repeated failures add back-off, and a short blip becomes a long outage. Liveness should only check things a restart of this container can fix; put dependency checks in readiness (or nowhere).
Q3What should a readiness probe check, and is it ever wrong to include a dependency?
A good answer coversThat this pod can serve right now: it has started, finished warming up and isn't overloaded. Including a dependency every pod shares means one outage makes every pod unready, so the service returns 503s with no pods behind it. That's sometimes what you want (fail fast and clearly); a service that can serve degraded responses should stay ready and handle the error instead.
Q4An app takes 90 s to start. How would you configure its probes?
A good answer coversAdd a startup probe with periodSeconds × failureThreshold comfortably above 90 s, say 10 s × 15 = 150 s. Liveness and readiness don't run until it passes, so liveness can keep a short, sensible period for catching hangs later. Avoid the old workaround of a huge initialDelaySeconds on liveness, which also delays detecting a real hang after a restart.
Q5Under heavy load your pods keep restarting, and the liveness timeout is 1 s. What is going on, and what would you change?
A good answer coversProbes queue behind real requests, so busy pods answer late and get killed as if they were dead. Each restart loses work and moves load onto the remaining pods, which then miss their checks too: a restart storm. Raise the timeout and failure threshold, answer health checks outside the request queue (a separate lightweight handler or port), and fix the real capacity problem.
Q6If readiness already removes broken pods from traffic, why have a liveness probe at all?
A good answer coversBecause readiness never repairs anything. A deadlocked or wedged process fails readiness forever and you quietly run with less capacity. Liveness restarts it. The two answer different questions, so many apps need both, but each must check the right thing.
